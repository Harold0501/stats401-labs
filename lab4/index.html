Result Analysis

I used a 1,200-row sample from Cardiff NLP's TweetTopic training split, a corpus of English tweets posted between September 2019 and August 2021 and manually assigned to one or more of 19 topics. The source already masks most account names and URLs.

My quality audit found no missing text, duplicate IDs, or invalid dates. I removed one record with no topic, collapsed whitespace, parsed dates, and standardized the multi-label topic strings. I also created a pruned TF-IDF matrix for text inspection. Sentiment comes from the lab's Twitter-RoBERTa model. Its positive, neutral, and negative probabilities determine the class, while the continuous score is P(positive) minus P(negative). These are model estimates, not ground-truth labels.

The chart compares each topic's sentiment composition and sorts by mean score by default. Family is the most positive topic (+0.71; 38 of 46 tweets classified positive), followed by food and dining (+0.64). News and social concern is closest to neutral overall (+0.06) and has the largest negative share among the high-volume topics: 86 of 331 tweets. Smaller topic groups should be interpreted cautiously.
