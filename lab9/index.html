Dataset Description

The provided dataset reports 2025 nominal GDP in billions of current U.S. dollars for the 50 largest economies. Values are based on the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025; observations may include staff estimates and can be revised.

GDP rows are joined to world boundaries by ISO-3 identifier. Countries outside the provided top-50 dataset are shown in neutral gray as no data.

04 · Design & comparison

Design and Comparison

In the choropleth, the United States and China are the darkest countries, but their existing land areas still dominate shape perception. Canada, Russia, and Australia remain physically prominent even when their GDP color is moderate. Europe appears as a dense cluster of dark and mid-tone countries. The choropleth preserves borders, coastline, adjacency, direction, and recognizable location, so it best supports questions about regional patterns and where high-GDP countries cluster.

In the cartogram, the United States and China become overwhelmingly large, while Germany, Japan, India, and the United Kingdom form a visible second tier. Large-land, lower-output countries such as Russia, Canada, and Australia shrink sharply. Compact economies such as Singapore, the Netherlands, and Switzerland gain presence. The cartogram distorts shape, distance, adjacency, and exact position, but its proportional areas better support magnitude comparison, ranking, and assessment of each economy's share of the top-50 total.

Moving from color to area changes GDP from an attribute painted onto geography into the organizing geometry itself. Economic scale becomes immediate, while the familiar map becomes harder to read. Linked highlighting restores correspondence without erasing that productive trade-off.
