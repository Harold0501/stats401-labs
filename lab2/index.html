Design Justification

This visualization uses two aligned views so the four variables remain readable without relying on a conventional scatterplot. Population, a ratio variable, is mapped to horizontal bar length because the shared zero baseline makes city magnitudes easy to compare. Temperature, an interval variable, is mapped to the horizontal position of a dot on a separate Celsius scale. Region, a nominal variable, is encoded with distinct colors that group cities without implying order. Development level, an ordinal variable, controls dot size with Low, Medium, and High produce progressively larger circles. Each row shares one city label, allowing viewers to connect its population bar and temperature marker. The paired layout should make broad patterns visible, for example, whether larger cities tend to be cooler, and how development levels and regions are distributed, while the tooltip provides exact values for closer inspection.
