Design Description

Why I chose these visual channels

Rectangle area encodes GDP because a treemap naturally communicates part-to-whole magnitude while preserving the continent-area-country hierarchy. A restrained diverging palette uses teal for Increase, warm gray for Unchanged, and coral for Decrease, making status immediately distinguishable without implying that color represents GDP size. White area boundaries and dark continent headers clarify the nested groups, and country labels appear only when space permits, while tooltips retain exact values and the full hierarchical path for every country.

How the segmentation strategies change readability

The squarified layout keeps rectangles closer to square, so labels fit more often and country areas are easier to compare across the entire world. The slice-dice layout alternates horizontal and vertical subdivision at successive depths. Its long, narrow bands make parent grouping and the hierarchy's branching structure more apparent, but small economies become thin strips with less room for text. Showing both views demonstrates that tiling changes readability and spatial organization, not the underlying GDP values or status encoding.
