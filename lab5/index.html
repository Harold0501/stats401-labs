Node-Link Design Description

District uses color for quick geographic grouping. Node area represents daily passengers, while circle, diamond, and square distinguish local, transfer, and terminal roles without competing for color. Route color and line pattern separate Metro, Express, and Shuttle service; thicker links mean longer travel time. Hovering isolates a station's neighbors, tooltips report exact attributes and degree, and dragging helps untangle crowded areas.

Matrix Design Description

A filled cell marks a direct route. Color preserves route type and darker opacity means longer travel. Colored labels retain district, and their shape markers retain station type. Ordering by station number exposes the near-diagonal chain from Station 1 through Station 41. Cells farther from the diagonal reveal shortcut routes. This order makes the network's generated sequence clear, while district comparisons become less direct.

Network questions and findings

Which stations appear central?

This question can be answered well by both of the visualizations. Hover highlighting reveals fourteen stations with the maximum degree of three: 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 21, 23, 25, 29, 31, 33, 34, and 38. No dominant hub exists.

Which districts are strongly connected?

This question is partially answered by both visualizations. Label colors show that most routes cross districts. East-South appears most often, followed by Central-North and East-West. Numeric ordering makes exact district comparison less immediate.

Where are transfer and terminal stations?

This question is well answered by both visualizations. Shape markers place both roles across the topology. Terminals 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 25, 31, and 34 are degree-three junctions, while terminals 43, 46, and 49 are isolated.

Which stations carry high volume?

This question is well answered by the node-link visualization. Node area identifies stations 50, 49, 48, 47, and 46 as highest-volume. Their peripheral or isolated positions show that volume does not imply degree.

Where are the longest direct trips?

This question is well answered by both visualizations. The thickest link and darkest cells connect North 7 to Central 31 in 16 minutes, followed by South 18-East 34 in 15 minutes and East 29-West 50 in 14 minutes.

Are route types concentrated?

This question is partially answered by the matrix visualization. Matrix colors are interleaved, so concentration is modest. Express peaks on East-West and Shuttle on South-East with 4 each, while Metro is broadly dispersed.
